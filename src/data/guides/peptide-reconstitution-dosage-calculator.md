# How to Reconstitute Peptides: Concentration Calculator, Unit Conversion Chart and Step-by-Step Guide

Reconstitution is where most handling mistakes happen. Not in storage, not in transport, but in the two minutes where someone adds water to a vial and then has to know exactly what concentration they have made.

The good news is that the whole thing reduces to one formula. Learn it once and it applies to every lyophilised peptide, from BPC-157 to retatrutide.

This guide covers the maths, the procedure, a concentration chart and a unit conversion chart, plus notes on specific compounds and on storage.

> **Important:** This guide explains reconstitution and concentration only. It does not give dosing advice. All STRIATA products are sold strictly for research purposes and are not intended for human or veterinary use.

## What is reconstitution?

Peptides are supplied as a lyophilised (freeze-dried) powder in a sterile, sealed vial. Reconstitution is the process of dissolving that powder in a suitable solvent to produce a solution of a known concentration.

The solvent is almost always **bacteriostatic water (BAC water)**, which contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol as a preservative. That preservative is what allows a reconstituted vial to remain usable for up to 30 days at 2–8 °C.

Sterile water for injection (SWFI) contains no preservative. A vial reconstituted with sterile water should be used within 24 hours. For any vial that will be drawn from more than once, BAC water is the correct choice.

## The master formula

Everything in reconstitution comes down to this:

> **Concentration (mg/mL) = Peptide mass (mg) ÷ BAC water added (mL)**

From that, two derived forms answer every other question:

> **Peptide in a given volume (mg) = Concentration (mg/mL) × Volume (mL)**
>
> **Volume (mL) = Units on a U-100 syringe ÷ 100**

### Understanding the U-100 syringe

A U-100 insulin syringe holds 1 mL in total. The barrel is graduated into 100 equal units, so **each unit equals 0.01 mL**. Once that clicks, the conversions stop being confusing.

| Syringe mark | Volume | Fraction of 1 mL |
|---|---|---|
| 10 units | 0.10 mL | One tenth |
| 20 units | 0.20 mL | One fifth |
| 25 units | 0.25 mL | One quarter |
| 50 units | 0.50 mL | One half |
| 100 units | 1.00 mL | Full syringe |

## Step-by-step reconstitution procedure

**1.** Wash your hands thoroughly and work on a clean, flat surface.

**2.** Remove both vials from refrigeration and allow the peptide vial and the BAC water to reach room temperature, around 5–10 minutes. This reduces the risk of thermal shock to the peptide.

**3.** Swab the rubber septum of both vials with a fresh 70% IPA swab. Allow 10 seconds to air-dry before inserting any needle.

**4.** Draw your chosen volume of BAC water into the syringe. For example, 1 mL to make 1 mg/mL from a 1 mg vial, or 2 mL to make 5 mg/mL from a 10 mg vial.

**5.** Insert the needle into the peptide vial at an angle so that the stream of water runs down the inside wall of the vial rather than landing directly on the powder cake. This prevents foaming and protects the peptide from degradation.

**6.** **Do not shake.** Gently roll the vial between your palms or swirl it slowly until the powder is fully dissolved. Most peptides dissolve within 30–60 seconds; some, notably TB-500, take one to two minutes.

**7.** Inspect the solution visually. It should be clear and colourless, or very faintly yellow for some compounds. Discard if cloudy, particulate or discoloured.

**8.** Label the vial immediately with the compound name, concentration in mg/mL, the date reconstituted, and your initials. Store at 2–8 °C.

> **Never use ultrasonic baths, vortex mixers, or vigorous shaking.** Peptide bonds are fragile and mechanical stress degrades potency. Gentle swirling only.

## Worked concentration examples

### Example A: BPC-157, 5 mg vial

BAC water added: 2.0 mL.

- Concentration = 5 mg ÷ 2.0 mL = **2.5 mg/mL**
- Each unit = 0.01 mL × 2.5 mg/mL = **25 mcg**
- 10 units (0.10 mL) contain **250 mcg**

### Example B: GHK-Cu, 5 mg vial

BAC water added: 5.0 mL.

- Concentration = 5 ÷ 5.0 = **1.0 mg/mL**
- Each unit = **10 mcg**
- A full 1 mL syringe contains **1 mg**

### Example C: Semaglutide, 2 mg vial, and an awkward number

BAC water added: 1.0 mL gives **2.0 mg/mL**, where 0.25 mg sits at 12.5 units, between two marks.

**Better approach:** reconstitute in 2 mL instead, giving **1.0 mg/mL**, and 0.25 mg sits on the 25-unit mark. Same amount, far easier to measure. This is exactly the situation where adding more water solves the problem.

### Example D: TB-500, 5 mg vial

BAC water added: 1.0 mL.

- Concentration = 5 ÷ 1.0 = **5.0 mg/mL**
- Each unit = **50 mcg**

TB-500 needs a longer dissolution time: one to two minutes of gentle swirling.

## Storage and stability after reconstitution

| State | Storage temperature | Shelf life | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lyophilised powder, sealed | Room temperature (15–25 °C) | To expiry date, typically 24+ months | Keep away from direct light and moisture |
| Lyophilised powder, unsealed | 2–8 °C refrigerated | Within 6 months | Do not freeze |
| Reconstituted in BAC water | 2–8 °C refrigerated | 28–30 days | Do not freeze; discard if cloudy |
| Reconstituted in sterile water | 2–8 °C refrigerated | Within 24 hours | Prepare only what you need immediately |
| Reconstituted, frozen in aliquots | −20 °C | Up to 3 months | Single freeze only; never refreeze after thawing |

> **Never boil, autoclave, or expose reconstituted peptide solutions to temperatures above 37 °C.** Heat permanently denatures peptide structure and cannot be undone.

Store reconstituted vials at the **back of the fridge, not in the door**. The door experiences a temperature spike every time the fridge is opened, and for GLP-1 class peptides in particular, that cycling meaningfully shortens usable life.

## Troubleshooting

| Issue | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Powder won't dissolve** | Allow more time and gently swirl or roll the vial. Some peptides, particularly TB-500, need 2–3 minutes. Never shake. |
| **Cloudy solution** | Do not use. May indicate contamination, precipitation, or degraded peptide. Discard. |
| **Floating particles** | Discard. Could be peptide aggregates or particulate contamination; neither can be filtered out. |
| **Blue or green tint** | Normal for GHK-Cu due to the copper content. Other peptides should be clear to faintly yellow. |
| **Foamy solution** | Caused by adding BAC water directly onto the powder. Discard if severely foamed; otherwise wait about 10 minutes for bubbles to settle. |
| **Unsure of concentration** | Discard and reconstitute fresh. Never estimate; always recalculate from the formula. |
| **Air bubble in syringe** | Tap the barrel gently and push the bubble out before reading the volume, or the measurement will be short. |

## Frequently asked questions

### How much BAC water should I add to my peptide vial?

There is no single correct answer. The amount of water only sets the concentration, and any volume from 1 to 3 mL is fine from a stability standpoint for most vials (glutathione and NAD+ need up to 10 mL). Pick the volume that gives a concentration that is easy to measure from, which usually means a round number such as 1, 2, 2.5 or 5 mg/mL.

### Can I use sterile water instead of bacteriostatic water?

Only if the entire vial will be used within 24 hours. Sterile water has no preservative, so any multi-use vial reconstituted with it becomes a contamination risk. For anything drawn from more than once, use BAC water.

### What happens if I shake the vial?

Shaking triggers fibrillar aggregation: the peptide chains clump together and the compound loses activity. This is irreversible. Once a peptide has aggregated, no amount of mixing, warming or filtering will restore it. Gentle swirling only.

### How long does a reconstituted peptide last?

Generally 28–30 days refrigerated in BAC water, though this varies by compound. GLP-1 class peptides such as tirzepatide and semaglutide sit at the shorter end of that window, and retatrutide should be used within 21 days given limited stability data. More robust compounds like BPC-157 and TB-500 tolerate longer. Label every vial with its reconstitution date so you are never guessing.

### Can I freeze a reconstituted vial?

Not in a normal fridge-freezer for routine use. Ice crystal formation disrupts peptide structure. Pre-aliquoted solution stored at −20 °C can hold for up to three months, but only as a single freeze; never refreeze after thawing.

### How do I work out how much peptide is in a given volume?

Multiply the concentration in mg/mL by the volume in mL. On a U-100 syringe, the volume in mL is the number of units divided by 100. So 20 units of a 2.5 mg/mL solution is 0.2 mL × 2.5 mg/mL = 0.5 mg. The unit conversion chart above covers the most common combinations.

### My vial has gone cloudy before the expiry window. What now?

Discard it and do not use it. Then consider the cause: cloudiness well inside the shelf life, with correct storage, may indicate a product quality issue rather than handling error.
